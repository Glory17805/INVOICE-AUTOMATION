What the offline reader misses

With no LLM configured, invoices are read by parsing the PDF's text layer. On the one vendor template in use today that works almost perfectly. This is an account of everything else — measured, not estimated.

Scans and photographs

No workaround offline

There is no OCR anywhere in the system. Every image format returns zero characters, as does a PDF that is a scan rather than generated text. The upload screen accepts .png, .jpg, .gif and .webp, so a photograph of a bill can be uploaded and comes back completely blank.

Invoices that print the label and value on one line

Common layout

The reader assumes a label sits on one line and its value on the next — the shape this vendor's software emits. A great many billing systems instead print Invoice No: INV-118    Date: 12-Jul-2026 on a single line. Almost nothing survives that.

It also recognises only a fixed vocabulary of labels. “Invoice No.” and “Invoice Number” are understood; “Bill No”, “Invoice #” and “Tax Invoice No” are not.

Reads the header, loses every figure

The totals block is only trusted when its own arithmetic closes to within five paise. That check is what stops the reader inventing numbers — but any extra line on the invoice breaks the sum, and the whole block is discarded.

One extra line on the bill and the money goes blank

Fails safe

Invoice number, date and party name still read. Every amount comes back empty, and posting is blocked on the missing taxable value.

Read wrong, and nothing said so

These two mattered most. Both produced plausible figures that passed every check in the system and would have posted to the workbook clean. Both are now caught.

Invoices carrying more than one tax rate were flattened into one

Fixed

A bill with rice at 5% and pipes at 18% was read as a single taxable value and a single tax figure. The two agree with each other perfectly, so the arithmetic check passed. Only the rate implied by dividing one by the other gives it away: 13.67%, which is not a GST rate that exists.

The fix adds a check that the derived rate is one of the seven statutory slabs, with half a percentage point of tolerance — wide enough to absorb an invoice's own rounding, narrow enough that two different slabs averaged together cannot land inside it.

5% and 18% goods on one bill
read as : taxable 3000.00   CGST 205.00   SGST 205.00   → rate 13.67%
truth   : two rows — 1000 @ 5% and 2000 @ 18%

before  : blocks *** NOTHING ***
after   : blocks rate_not_statutory

Reverse-charge bills were filed on the ordinary register

Fixed

Detection required the answer to follow immediately after the words “reverse charge”. The wording the GST portal itself uses — “Whether tax payable on reverse charge basis” with Yes on the next line — did not match, so those bills went to the normal register and the tax liability landed on the wrong party.

The fix looks for the first answer within a short window after the phrase. The first answer wins, so an explicit No is honoured rather than skipped over in search of a Yes further down the page, and a document that names reverse charge but answers nothing still reads as false.

wordings now handled
yes   Reverse Charge: Yes
yes   Reverse charge - applicable
yes   Whether tax payable on reverse charge basis ↵ Yes
yes   REVERSE CHARGE APPLICABLE

no    Reverse Charge: No
no    Reverse charge - Not Applicable
no    Whether tax payable on reverse charge basis ↵ N/A
no    Reverse charge            (named, unanswered)
no    Reverse Charge: No ↵ E-way bill required: Yes

Smaller misreads, still open

Known
  • Only the first HSN code is captured. The line-items list is always empty, so a multi-item bill files under one code.
  • “Credit note” is matched anywhere in the text — a mention in your terms and conditions could misclassify a document.
  • The supplier GSTIN is taken as the first one that is not the customer's, so a transporter's or bank's GSTIN in the footer could win.

What protects you

Everything unreadable is stopped before the workbook

Verified
  • A blank taxable value, a missing date, a missing party name and a failed GSTIN checksum each block posting on their own.
  • Nothing auto-posts. The auto_post_clean setting exists in the settings file but is read by no code, so the toggle currently does nothing.
  • Overriding a blocked invoice is recorded on its history, so the decision is visible to an auditor.

The exposure was entirely the two silent cases above, which is why they were the two worth fixing.

Where this leaves you

If invoices only ever arrive from this one vendor's software, offline is genuinely adequate. The reader handles that layout completely, and the two ways it could have been quietly wrong are now caught.

If bills will arrive from other suppliers, expect gaps. A different billing system's layout — particularly one printing labels and values on the same line — may yield almost nothing, and a scan or a photograph will yield nothing at all. Those documents are flagged rather than mis-filed, but somebody has to type them in.

The LLM path remains the answer for anything outside the known template. Nothing in this report argues against staying offline today; it argues for knowing exactly which invoice will need a human.
