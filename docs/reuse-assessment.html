Ira Innovations · Reuse assessment

Generator vs Reader

Whether the Proposal–Invoice Automation repository can be reused for the GST invoice automation, what overlaps, and what is worth taking from it anyway.

1 September 2026 Theirs maliksudais24/Proposal-invoice-automation- @ b01551a Ours gst-automation @ f8ce469
The answer

No — they solve the opposite problem, and the code is not licensed for reuse

Both projects have "invoice" in the name and there the resemblance ends. Theirs writes invoices out: you type a client and some line items, an LLM drafts a cover paragraph, and it renders a PDF. Ours reads invoices in: a document arrives, the system extracts it, decides which of four GST registers it belongs on and how it is taxed, and appends a row to the client's existing workbook.

There is no GST in their project at all. Tax is a single float, tax = subtotal × tax_rate, with a default currency of $. No CGST/SGST/IGST split, no GSTIN, no place of supply, no registers, no return period. That is not a shortcoming of their work — it is a generator for a consultancy's own billing, and it does that job.

Separately and independently: the repository carries no licence file. Under default copyright that means all rights reserved. It can be read and learned from; it cannot be copied into a commercial client deliverable without the author's written permission.

01 What each one actually does

The clearest way to see it is the direction the data flows.

Theirs — a generator

Invoice & Proposal Generator Agent

You supply the facts. It produces the document.

a person types client + services + rates ↓ validate (schema.py) total up (calculations.py) retrieve (rag_store.py, TF-IDF) write (llm_writer.py) render (pdf_builder.py) ↓ a PDF to send

Built for a consultancy issuing its own proposals. The stated problem is that drafting one by hand takes 20–30 minutes.

Ours — a reader

GST Invoice Automation

A document arrives. It works out what it is and files it.

an invoice arrives upload / email / scan ↓ split (one file, many invoices) extract (Gemini or Claude) classify (sale / purchase / CN / RCM) apply GST (intra vs inter-state) validate (GSTIN, arithmetic, dupes) ↓ a row in the client's workbook

Built for a filing obligation. The problem is the manual typing, and the CGST/SGST-vs-IGST judgment call on every line.

An invoice is the output of one system and the input of the other. Nothing in the middle of theirs — the part that carries the value — has an equivalent in ours, and nothing in the middle of ours has an equivalent in theirs.

03 Worth taking anyway

Three ideas are genuinely good and would improve our system. All three are approaches to copy, not files — see the licence note.

01

PDF generation is the half we do not have

Idea

Ira Innovations issues sales invoices — that is what GSTR-1 is a register of. We read those invoices back out of PDFs their billing software produced. If the goal were ever to issue them from this system instead, their pdf_builder.py is exactly the missing stage, and it is the smallest, most self-contained thing in their repository.

Worth raising as a product question rather than an engineering one: is issuing invoices in scope, or does the billing software keep that job?

02

TF-IDF as an offline retrieval store

Idea

They use scikit-learn's TF-IDF vectoriser with cosine similarity as a vector store, deliberately avoiding an embeddings API. It is a sound choice: no network, no key, no per-call cost, and for a corpus of a few dozen documents the quality difference against real embeddings barely registers.

The application here is different but real. Our extraction currently reads every invoice cold. Grounding it against previously-confirmed invoices from the same supplier — this vendor's layout, this GSTIN, the HSN codes they usually bill — would give the reader a prior, and would give a reviewer a reason when something looks unusual.

03

A no-LLM fallback that still produces something usable

Shared

Their _fallback_summary() writes a plain, correct cover paragraph with no model involved, so the system degrades to "less elegant" rather than "broken". We reached the same conclusion independently with the offline reader, and it has already earned its place twice — once when the Anthropic account ran out of credit, once before a Gemini key existed.

Convergence on this from two directions is worth noting: it is the right shape for any system with a model in the middle of it.

04 The licence, plainly

Before copying anything

The repository has no licence file. Not MIT, not Apache, not "unlicensed" in the permissive sense — simply absent. Under the Berne Convention and Indian copyright law alike, the default for published work with no licence is all rights reserved. Being publicly readable on GitHub grants the right to view and fork within GitHub, not to reuse in a product.

This matters here specifically because the output is a commercial deliverable for a paying client, filing that client's statutory returns. Copying unlicensed code into it would put a copyright question inside a compliance system, which is the worst possible place for one.

If you want to use their code: ask the author to add a licence, or for written permission. It is a reasonable request and costs them a two-line file. Until then, the ideas in section 3 can be implemented from scratch — TF-IDF retrieval and PDF rendering are both standard techniques, and reimplementing them from the documented approach is entirely clean.

05 Recommendation

Do not adopt or merge their project. It aims at a different problem, has no GST model, no tests, and no licence permitting reuse. Adopting it would mean discarding the workbook writer, the GST rules, the four registers, the accounts and audit trail, and 241 tests pinned to this client's own figures — in exchange for a PDF generator we do not currently need.

Do take two ideas from it. Supplier-history grounding for extraction is the one I would actually pursue; it addresses a real weakness, in that every invoice is currently read with no memory of the last one from the same vendor. PDF generation is worth parking until someone decides whether issuing invoices is in scope at all.

One thing worth checking with whoever sent you the link. If the expectation was that this repository does what ours does, it does not. If the expectation was that the two are complementary halves — one issuing, one filing — that is closer to true, and a conversation about scope rather than about code.
